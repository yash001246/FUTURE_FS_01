Our Story

Roast Line started at a card table with a one-kilo sample roaster and a notebook full of curves that didn't work yet.

Six years on, the notebook is a wall of logs behind the counter — one line per batch, First Crack marked in red, drop time circled. We still roast small, still taste every batch blind, and still hold back anything that doesn't earn its shelf.

What comes out the other end is coffee that tastes the same way twice: the way we meant it to.

Bean to Cup

How a bag gets made.

The same four stages, every batch — timed, tasted, and written down before it ever reaches the counter.

01

Source

We buy direct from farms and cooperatives we've cupped ourselves, at prices set above commodity floor.

Weeks 1–6, before arrival
02

Roast & log

Each batch is roasted to a plotted curve — First Crack and drop time recorded by hand on the wall log.

11–13 minutes
03

Rest

Beans off-gas in sealed bins before bagging — pulled too early, a shot tastes flat no matter how good the roast.

48 hours minimum
04

Cup & pour

We cup every batch blind before it's approved for the counter — anything off gets pulled from the shelf.

Daily, 8am
